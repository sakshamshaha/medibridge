MEDICINE BLISTER / STRIP OCR Complete implementation specification for a production-oriented medicine identification feature Open-source OCR + computer vision + barcode + medicine database + validation + model training Representative difficult input: medicine blister/strip photograph with small reflective foil text. Prepared: 26 August 2026 0. Executive answer to the training question Yes. The OCR model can be trained/fine-tuned, and for this project that is one of the highest-value improvements you can make. The recommended path is not to train a giant VLM from scratch. Instead, start from PP-OCRv6 pretrained weights and fine-tune the text-detection and text-recognition components on your own medicine-strip images. PaddleOCR explicitly documents vertical-scene fine-tuning for detection and recognition and recommends starting from pretrained weights. Its documentation says a small amount of real vertical-scene data can materially improve accuracy; it gives &gt;=500 detection samples and &gt;=5,000 recognition samples as practical starting points. [1] The most important distinction is that detection and recognition are different learning problems. Detection learns where the tiny text is. Recognition learns what the text says. For medicine blister packs, you should train them separately, then measure end-to-end medicine-identification accuracy. Goal and non-goals 1.1 Primary goal Given a mobile-phone photograph of a medicine blister strip, tablet strip, foil pack, or similar medicine packaging, identify the medicine reliably enough for the application to present a candidate medicine name, strength, manufacturer and supporting evidence, while refusing to guess when confidence is too low. 1.2 Secondary goals Recover visible medicine name and strength from very small text. Recover manufacturer when possible. Recover batch/lot and expiry when readable, but treat them as secondary fields. Decode EAN/UPC/DataMatrix/other barcodes when present. Handle perspective distortion, mild curvature, blur, glare, low contrast and foil reflections. Support multilingual text that the selected OCR model can recognize; specifically test English plus the languages actually present in your target market. Use multiple pieces of evidence instead of trusting a single OCR string. Provide deterministic diagnostics explaining why an image was rejected or why the identification is low confidence. 1.3 Non-goals Do not treat OCR as a medical prescribing or dosing engine. Do not infer a medicine solely from an uncertain fuzzy string match. Do not claim that a generic OCR benchmark proves performance on blister foil; build and publish an in-domain evaluation set. Do not make the first version dependent on a 35B document parser when a specialized OCR stack can handle the bulk of requests. 2. Why blister/strip OCR is a special problem A medicine blister is not a normal document page. The text can be tiny, repeated, curved, partially occluded by pill cavities, printed on reflective foil, stamped or embossed, photographed at a severe angle, or captured at insufficient resolution. The useful text may occupy only a small fraction of the image. The camera can also introduce motion blur, focus blur, lens distortion, white-balance shifts, compression artifacts and specular highlights. Model selection 4.1 Primary model: PP-OCRv6 Medium PP-OCRv6 is the latest generation in PaddleOCR as of this specification. The official documentation describes tiny, small and medium tiers from 1.5M to 34.5M parameters. The medium/small models support a unified 50-language model, and the project highlights improvements in specialized text scenarios such as industrial characters. The official PP-OCRv6 medium recognition configuration uses a PPLCNetV4 backbone with SVTR-LCNet-style recognition components. [2][5] Property Why it matters here 34.5M parameters Small enough for practical server inference compared with a giant VLM. Specialized OCR architecture Better fit than a generic VLM for raw text detection/recognition. Separate detection and recognition Allows domain-specific training for blister images. 50-language unified support in medium/small Useful if target medicines contain multiple Latin-script languages. Official training configs available Enables direct fine-tuning instead of rebuilding the pipeline. [6] Do not choose the model purely from vendor benchmark claims. Your selection criterion should ultimately be medicine-level identification accuracy on your own held-out blister dataset. 4.2 Secondary model: PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 is an Apache-2.0 0.9B image-to-text/document parsing model. The official model card reports a 96.33% OmniDocBench v1.6 score and provides direct PaddleOCR integration. [4] It is useful here as a difficult-case verifier because it can reason over the image rather than only performing line-level OCR, but the project should not assume that a document benchmark translates directly into blister-pack accuracy. Recommended use: send only a focused blister/foil crop or the best processed image, ask for structured fields, forbid guessing, and compare its result with PP-OCRv6 rather than replacing the specialized OCR engine. 4.3 Alternative models to benchmark Candidate Use in this project Decision GLM-OCR Lightweight VLM OCR alternative Benchmark as fallback candidate; not first choice without in-domain tests dots.mocr / dots.ocr Multilingual/document/scene text alternative Benchmark for difficult images if available in your deployment constraints Infinity-Parser2-Pro Heavy document parser Do not make it the core OCR path; retain as research/verification option only if it proves useful Tesseract Baseline / debugging Keep as a baseline; not the primary engine 5. Camera and capture UX 5.1 Live capture guidance The camera UI should actively help the user produce OCR-friendly images. A scanning rectangle should occupy a useful portion of the preview, and the app should display short instructions such as “Move closer”, “Reduce reflection”, “Hold steady”, or “Place strip inside frame”. 5.2 Capture quality gate Before inference, compute a quality vector. The exact thresholds must be tuned on your own camera dataset. quality = {   sharpness: laplacian_variance(image),   brightness: mean_luma(image),   glare_ratio: saturated_highlight_area(image),   coverage: text_or_strip_area_ratio(image),   perspective: estimated_skew_or_quad_distortion(image),   resolution: min(width, height), } if quality.sharpness &lt; SHARPNESS_MIN:     reject("Image is blurry") elif quality.glare_ratio &gt; GLARE_MAX:     prompt("Move away from direct light") elif quality.coverage &lt; COVERAGE_MIN:     prompt("Move closer") else:     proceed() 5.3 Multi-frame capture Instead of trusting one photo, capture a short burst of 3-5 frames. Rank frames by blur, glare, text/strip coverage and exposure. Run the OCR stack on the top 1-2 frames or use the best frame for the initial pass. This can produce a larger real-world gain than swapping between similarly strong OCR models. 6. Image preprocessing pipeline 6.1 Stage A - image normalization Decode the original image without unnecessary recompression. Correct orientation using the camera metadata and optional orientation classification. Downscale only if the source is excessively large; preserve enough pixels for tiny text. Normalize color space and keep an untouched original for audit/reprocessing. 6.2 Stage B - ROI detection Detect the medicine strip/blister or a relevant packaging region. The first implementation may use a simple object detector or user-guided crop. A later version can train a lightweight “medicine-pack ROI detector” using the same captured dataset. 6.3 Stage C - geometric rectification If four corners or a stable quadrilateral can be estimated, use a homography to rectify the image. For curved foil, use an optional nonlinear dewarping stage. Do not force dewarping on already-flat images when it introduces distortions. 6.4 Stage D - multiple image variants Variant Purpose RGB/original Preserve model-native evidence and color clues. Grayscale + local contrast Improve faint print on foil. CLAHE Improve local contrast when illumination is uneven. Unsharp/high-pass Recover edges when the source is mildly soft. Illumination corrected Reduce broad reflections/brightness gradients. Upscaled crop Give tiny characters more pixel support; do not invent information. Do not threshold everything into pure black/white. Foil and embossed text frequently contain useful grayscale gradients that binarization can destroy. 7. Text detection and recognition design 7.1 Detection The detector should find text regions, ideally as polygons when text is rotated or slightly curved. Detection is where domain fine-tuning often produces a major gain because generic detectors can miss very small or low-contrast foil text. 7.2 Recognition The recognizer receives a normalized text-line crop and predicts the transcription. Medicine text tends to contain short words, digits, decimals, units, punctuation and abbreviations, so the character distribution differs from general web/document corpora. 7.3 Keep detection and recognition metrics separate Metric Meaning Why you need it Detection precision Fraction of predicted text regions that are correct Find false detections on logos/foil textures. Detection recall Fraction of annotated text regions found Critical for tiny missing fields. Recognition accuracy Exact line-level correctness Direct measure of OCR quality. CER Character error rate Useful for tiny strings and partial errors. WER Word error rate Useful for brand/manufacturer names. End-to-end text accuracy Correctly detected and recognized lines Measures the combined OCR stack. Medicine identification accuracy Correct final medicine candidate The most important product KPI. 8. Barcode branch Barcode scanning should run in parallel with OCR, not after OCR failure. Pharmaceutical packaging can use GS1 DataMatrix and other identifiers. GS1 describes the healthcare use of DataMatrix and notes that GS1 identifiers can carry product identifiers such as GTIN plus attributes such as batch/lot, expiry and serial number. [7] Use ZXing-C++ as the initial open-source reader. The project supports UPC/EAN, Code 128, Data Matrix ECC200 and other retail/industrial/matrix symbologies and provides C++ plus multiple bindings. [8] image   -&gt; grayscale / contrast variants   -&gt; barcode detector   -&gt; ZXing-C++   -&gt; decoded symbology + payload   -&gt; normalize payload   -&gt; lookup product identifier   -&gt; use barcode evidence in final score A barcode match can be stronger than OCR text. However, do not assume every barcode payload is a medicine database key without understanding the symbology and country/manufacturer data structure. 9. Medicine database and candidate generation 9.1 Required fields Field Example Importance Canonical medicine/brand name Risperdal Primary Generic/active ingredient Risperidone Primary/secondary Strength 2 mg Primary Dosage form tablet Secondary Manufacturer Janssen-Cilag Strong corroborating evidence Aliases Risperdal, Risperdal 2mg Normalization Barcode/GTIN where available product identifier Strong evidence Country/market India Disambiguation Packaging descriptors 20 tablets oral Supporting evidence 9.2 Candidate search normalized_ocr = normalize_text(raw_ocr) name_candidates = search_brand_names(normalized_ocr) strength_candidates = parse_strength(raw_ocr) manufacturer_candidates = search_manufacturers(raw_ocr) barcode_candidates = barcode_lookup(decoded_barcode) final_candidates = rank_candidates(     name_candidates,     strength_candidates,     manufacturer_candidates,     barcode_candidates ) The search layer should combine exact matching, normalized matching and conservative fuzzy matching. RapidFuzz or a database trigram index can be used for candidate generation, but candidate acceptance must be evidence-based. 10. Medicine-specific text normalization OCR often confuses visually similar characters. Common examples include O/0, I/l/1, S/5, B/8 and punctuation around strengths. Normalization should be context-aware. raw:        "RispredaI 2mg" normalize:  "rispredai 2 mg" contextual: compare against medicine dictionary result:     "Risperdal 2 mg" (candidate, not blindly accepted) Normalize Unicode and whitespace. Collapse repeated spaces and punctuation. Normalize common unit spellings (mg, g, mcg, mL, %, IU) without changing numeric values. Do not globally replace “I” with “l” or “0” with “O”; use contextual candidate matching. Keep the original OCR string for auditability. Store token-level confidence if available. 11. Confidence and evidence engine The application needs a dedicated confidence layer. Model confidence alone is insufficient because a high-confidence OCR string can still correspond to the wrong drug. score = (     w_ocr * ocr_confidence   + w_name * name_similarity   + w_strength * strength_match   + w_mfr * manufacturer_match   + w_barcode * barcode_match   + w_quality * image_quality   + w_agreement * model_agreement ) if barcode_exact and trusted_product_match:     score += barcode_bonus if score &gt;= HIGH_THRESHOLD:     status = "high_confidence" elif score &gt;= REVIEW_THRESHOLD:     status = "needs_confirmation" else:     status = "retake_image" The exact formula is not sacred. Train/calibrate it using a held-out dataset. If the application ever displays a medicine recommendation, the product layer should expose why the candidate was selected: e.g. “name matched 98%, strength matched 100%, barcode matched exact”. 12. Difficult-case fallback with PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 12.1 Trigger conditions PP-OCRv6 confidence below threshold. No usable medicine-name candidate. Two leading candidates are close in score. OCR results disagree strongly across image variants. Detection found text but recognition is inconsistent. Image contains unusual layout or irregular text that the primary pipeline handles poorly. 12.2 Structured prompt Read only visible pharmaceutical packaging text. Return JSON only: {   "medicine_name": string|null,   "strength": string|null,   "manufacturer": string|null,   "batch": string|null,   "expiry": string|null } Rules: - Do not guess missing characters. - Do not infer text that is not visible. - Use null for unreadable fields. - Preserve exact visible spelling when possible. 12.3 Cross-model agreement If PP-OCRv6 and PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 agree on medicine name and strength, increase confidence. If they disagree, do not average the strings; treat the discrepancy as a review signal and run database candidate ranking. 13. Can we train the OCR model? Yes - detailed training plan 13.1 What exactly gets trained? The recommended path is fine-tuning, not training from zero. PaddleOCR provides separate training/evaluation/inference modules and separate configurations for detection and recognition. The PP-OCRv6 medium recognition config currently exposes pretrained weights, a 100-epoch baseline schedule, SVTR_LCNet-based recognition with a PPLCNetV4 medium backbone, multi-scale training and augmentations; the detector has its own configuration. Keep the raw crop, corrected/rectified crop, label, source image id and field type. If the same medicine strip was photographed 50 times, all 50 variants should stay in one split. A better rule is to split by product/packaging family and by physical capture session/device where possible. Synthetic data generation Synthetic data is extremely useful for increasing character diversity and stress-testing the exact phenomena that make blister OCR hard. It should augment, not replace, real images. 14.1 Synthetic text crops Render medicine names, strengths, units, manufacturers and batch-like strings using fonts approximating the target packaging. Vary character spacing, font weight, print height and tracking. Apply perspective transforms, local warps and mild curvature. Add blur, motion blur and compression artifacts. Add foil-like background textures and specular highlights. Vary brightness, white balance and color temperature. Add partial occlusion and pill-cavity shadows. Generate hard negatives such as logos, symbols, decorative lines and foil texture that should not be recognized as text. 14.2 Synthetic full-pack images Take product layouts or generic packaging templates and composite the rendered text/foil into realistic scenes. Preserve the exact ground-truth text so the resulting data is automatically labeled. 15. False-positive safety design The most dangerous failure is not “could not read”. It is “read the wrong medicine and displayed it confidently”. Therefore, design the system to be asymmetric: uncertain outputs should fail closed. Require a strong name match plus strength agreement whenever strength is visible. Use manufacturer as corroboration, not as sole proof. Prefer exact/near-exact barcode evidence when a trusted lookup exists. If two medicines have similar names, require stronger evidence. Never invent missing characters simply to complete a database name. Show a confirmation state when evidence is mixed. Log the evidence components used for the decision. 20. Compute recommendations 25.1 Development Workload Practical hardware starting point PP-OCRv6 inference CPU is feasible; NVIDIA GPU improves throughput. PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 fallback Dedicated GPU strongly preferred. Recognizer fine-tuning 8-16 GB VRAM is a useful starting range; exact memory depends on batch size/input resolution. Detector fine-tuning Prefer 16 GB+ VRAM for comfortable experimentation; use smaller batch size/gradient accumulation if needed. Mobile app captures best frame and uploads JPEG/HEIC/PNG within a configured size limit. 2.  Backend runs quality checks and preprocessing. 3.  PP-OCRv6 runs on CPU/GPU. 4.  Fallback VLM runs only for uncertain cases. 5.  Medicine DB resolves candidates. 6.  API returns structured result and confidence state. 26.2 Version 2 - hybrid edge/server 1.  Run blur/glare/quality gate on-device. 2.  Optionally run PP-OCRv6 small or another edge model on-device. 3.  Upload only when confidence is low or barcode lookup fails. 4.  Keep PaddleOCR-VL-1.6 server-side. 27. Latency optimization Use a bounded input resolution; do not feed 20+ megapixel photos to every model. Crop early; OCR the relevant region rather than the entire photo when possible. Run barcode detection in parallel with OCR. Cache medicine candidate searches by normalized name/strength. Batch requests when processing multiple frames or crops on a GPU. Do not invoke the VLM fallback unless the decision really needs it. Use model warm-up and persistent workers. 28. Retake decision behavior. 29.2 Golden-image tests Maintain a fixed set of representative images and expected structured results. Every model/preprocessing change must run against the set. Store both positive and failure cases. 29.3 Regression rule A new OCR model is not allowed to ship because average OCR accuracy improved. It must not regress medicine-level identification or materially increase dangerous false positives. 30. Concrete first milestone - build this before training The fastest way to start is to implement the inference pipeline with pretrained models before collecting a huge dataset. This tells you which stage is actually failing. 1.  Create a 100-300 image pilot set of real blister/strip photos. 2.  Run PP-OCRv6 Medium on original, contrast, sharpened and rectified variants. 3.  Add barcode decoding in parallel. 4.  Build candidate search against a small curated medicine dataset. 5.  Add confidence scoring and a hard “retake image” path. 6.  Log every failure and classify it into detection, recognition, preprocessing, database or scoring failure. 7.  Only then decide where fine-tuning will produce the biggest return. 32. Fine-tuning roadmap after the pilot Week / milestone model M0:     100-300 real images -&gt; baseline M1:     +500 detection annotations -&gt; detector experiment M2:     +5,000 recognition crops -&gt; recognizer experiment M3:     hard-case augmentation -&gt; synthetic expansion M4:     2k-5k full photos + 10k-50k crops -&gt; strong v1 M5+:    production failures -&gt; continuous domain adaptation These are project-planning milestones, not fixed schedules. The important sequence is baseline -&gt; measure -&gt; annotate -&gt; fine-tune -&gt; measure again. 33. What not to do Do not run a giant VLM on every image and call that the OCR system. Do not assume document benchmark scores transfer to blister foil. Do not crop away context before you have stored the original image and coordinates. Do not train only on clean screenshots or downloaded product images if deployment uses phone photos. Do not randomly split repeated photos of the same strip across train and test. Do not let fuzzy search silently convert uncertain OCR into a definitive drug name. Do not evaluate only average OCR accuracy; evaluate medicine-level false identifications. Do not retrain from scratch unless you have a research reason and substantial data/compute. 34. Direct answer: can we train the OCR model? Yes - and the project should plan for it from the beginning. The most practical route is to fine-tune PP-OCRv6 Medium’s detector and recognizer on your own annotated blister/strip data. PaddleOCR provides explicit detection and recognition training configurations, and its vertical-scene fine-tuning documentation is specifically intended for cases where a generic pretrained model is not enough. [5][10] Start with the pretrained model. Do not immediately change the architecture. First establish a baseline on your own test set. Then fine-tune recognition with real text-line crops. After that, fine-tune detection with full-image text polygons. Add realistic foil/glare/perspective synthetic augmentation. Keep a clean held-out test set and compare every new model against the baseline. If your dataset eventually becomes large enough, you can go beyond simple fine-tuning: train a medicine-specific text detector, a specialized recognizer, or a lightweight candidate-ranking model. But those should be second-stage improvements, not the starting point. 35.
